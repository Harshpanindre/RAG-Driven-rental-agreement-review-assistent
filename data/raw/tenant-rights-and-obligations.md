# Karnataka Tenant Rights and Landlord Obligations

## Overview

Rental laws in Karnataka are primarily governed by the **Karnataka Rent Act, 1999** (also referred to as the Karnataka Rent Control Act), which replaced the earlier Karnataka Rent Control Act, 1961. Additionally, the **Transfer of Property Act, 1882** and the **Indian Contract Act, 1872** provide supplementary legal frameworks. The central **Model Tenancy Act, 2021** has been recommended for adoption by states, and Karnataka has been considering aligning its laws accordingly.

---

## 1. Security Deposits

### Maximum Deposit Amounts

Under the Karnataka Rent Act, 1999, the security deposit is regulated as follows:

- **Residential premises**: The security deposit shall not exceed **ten months' rent**.
- In practice, landlords in Bengaluru and other urban areas of Karnataka commonly demand **10 months' rent** as a security deposit, which is legally permissible under the Act.
- The **Model Tenancy Act, 2021** (if adopted by Karnataka) recommends capping the security deposit at a maximum of **two months' rent** for residential properties and **six months' rent** for commercial properties.

### Allowable Deductions

A landlord may deduct from the security deposit only for:

1. **Unpaid rent**: Any rent remaining unpaid at the time the tenant vacates.
2. **Unpaid utility charges**: Electricity, water, and maintenance charges owed by the tenant.
3. **Damage to the property**: Cost of repairing damages beyond **normal wear and tear** caused by the tenant.
4. **Breach of agreement terms**: Costs incurred due to the tenant violating specific terms of the rental agreement.

### Return of Deposit

- The landlord must return the security deposit **within one month** of the tenant vacating the premises, after deducting any lawful amounts.
- If the landlord fails to return the deposit within the stipulated period, the tenant may approach the **Rent Authority** or file a civil suit for recovery.
- Under the Model Tenancy Act, 2021, the deposit must be refunded within **two months** of vacating, along with an itemized list of deductions.
- Non-return of security deposit is one of the **most common rental disputes** in Karnataka, particularly in Bengaluru.

### Interest on Security Deposit

- The Karnataka Rent Act does **not explicitly mandate** the payment of interest on the security deposit.
- However, if the rental agreement includes a clause for interest on the deposit, the landlord is bound by it.
- The Model Tenancy Act recommends interest at a rate to be prescribed by the state government.

## 2. Habitability and Maintenance Standards

### Landlord's Obligation to Maintain

Under the Karnataka Rent Act, 1999, and general principles of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882, the landlord is responsible for:

- Maintaining the **structural integrity** of the building, including walls, roof, foundation, and load-bearing structures.
- Ensuring **essential services** such as water supply, sanitation, and drainage are in working order.
- Maintaining **common areas** including staircases, corridors, lifts (elevators), parking areas, and compound walls.
- Carrying out **major repairs** to plumbing, electrical wiring, and sewage systems.
- Ensuring compliance with **local municipal building codes** and safety standards set by the BBMP (Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike) or relevant local body.

### Tenant's Maintenance Responsibilities

The tenant is generally responsible for:

- **Day-to-day maintenance** and minor repairs such as replacing fuses, taps, and bulbs.
- Maintaining the premises in a **clean and sanitary** condition.
- Not making any **structural alterations** without the landlord's written consent.
- Reporting any **major damage or defect** to the landlord promptly.
- Paying for repairs necessitated by the tenant's own **negligence or misuse**.

### Remedies for Failure to Maintain

If the landlord fails to maintain the property in habitable condition:

1. **Written notice**: The tenant should issue a written notice to the landlord specifying the defects and requesting repairs within a reasonable period.
2. **Self-repair and deduction**: If the landlord fails to act, the tenant may carry out essential repairs and deduct the cost from the rent, provided the tenant can prove the repairs were necessary and the landlord was given reasonable notice.
3. **Complaint to Rent Authority**: The tenant may file a complaint with the **Rent Authority** constituted under the Karnataka Rent Act.
4. **Municipal complaint**: The tenant may report uninhabitable conditions to the **BBMP** or relevant municipal authority, which can issue notices to the landlord.
5. **Withholding rent**: In extreme cases of uninhabitability, courts have recognized the tenant's right to withhold rent, though this should be done with legal advice.

## 3. Landlord's Right of Entry

### General Rule

Under Karnataka law and the Indian Easements Act, 1882, a tenant has the right to **peaceful and undisturbed enjoyment** of the rented premises. The landlord may enter the premises only under specific circumstances:

- **Emergency**: Such as fire, flooding, gas leak, or structural collapse — no prior notice required.
- **Inspection and repairs**: The landlord may enter for necessary inspections or to carry out repairs with **prior written notice** (typically 24 hours, or as specified in the rental agreement).
- **Showing the premises**: To prospective tenants or buyers, with prior notice and at a mutually agreed time.
- **With the tenant's consent**: At any time if the tenant explicitly agrees.

### Prohibited Conduct

- The landlord may **not enter the premises at unreasonable hours** without the tenant's permission.
- **Repeated uninvited entries** may constitute harassment and the tenant may seek relief under Section 441 of the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023** (criminal trespass, replacing the earlier IPC Section 441).
- The landlord cannot **disconnect essential services** (water, electricity) or remove doors/windows to force the tenant to vacate — this constitutes an **illegal eviction**.

## 4. Anti-Harassment and Protection of Tenants

### Prohibited Landlord Actions

Under the Karnataka Rent Act, 1999, and criminal law, a landlord may not:

- **Harass the tenant** to compel vacation of the premises.
- **Cut off essential services** such as water, electricity, or gas supply.
- **Lock out the tenant** or change the locks without legal proceedings.
- **Remove the tenant's belongings** from the premises.
- **Threaten or intimidate** the tenant or the tenant's family members.

### Legal Remedies for Harassment

- The tenant may file a **police complaint** under relevant sections of the BNS, 2023.
- The tenant may seek an **injunction** from the civil court to restrain the landlord from harassing activities.
- The tenant may file a complaint before the **Rent Authority** for protection under the Karnataka Rent Act.
- In cases of extreme harassment, the tenant may seek a **restraining order** from the court.

## 5. Rent Payment and Receipts

### Rent Due Date

- Rent is due on the date specified in the **rental agreement**, typically the **1st of each month**.
- If no date is specified, rent is payable at the **end of each month** as per the Transfer of Property Act, 1882.

### Mode of Payment

- Rent may be paid by **cash, cheque, bank transfer (NEFT/RTGS/UPI), or demand draft** as agreed between the parties.
- The landlord must issue a **written receipt** for every rent payment received in cash.
- It is advisable for tenants to always pay rent through **traceable digital modes** to maintain a clear record.

### Rent Receipts and HRA Claims

- Rent receipts are essential for tenants claiming **House Rent Allowance (HRA) exemption** under Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
- If the annual rent exceeds **₹1,00,000**, the tenant must obtain the **landlord's PAN number** for tax purposes.
- The landlord is legally obligated to provide rent receipts upon request.

### Non-Payment of Rent

- If the tenant defaults on rent, the landlord must issue a **notice demanding payment** before initiating eviction proceedings.
- Under the Karnataka Rent Act, the landlord must provide at least **15 days' notice** for the tenant to pay the arrears before filing for eviction.

## 6. Discrimination Protections

### Constitutional Protections

Under the **Constitution of India**, Articles 14, 15, and 21 guarantee:

- **Right to equality** — No person shall be discriminated against on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth.
- **Right to life and dignity** — Includes the right to adequate housing.

### Common Forms of Discrimination in Karnataka Rentals

While there is no specific anti-discrimination statute for rental housing in Karnataka, the following practices are widespread and may be challenged legally:

- Refusal to rent to tenants based on **religion, caste, dietary habits** (e.g., non-vegetarians), or **marital status**.
- Imposing different rent or deposit amounts based on the tenant's **community or region of origin**.
- Refusing to rent to **single women or unmarried couples**.
- Discriminatory rules regarding **pets, cooking practices, or cultural activities**.

### Legal Recourse

- Tenants facing discrimination may file a complaint under the **Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989** if caste-based discrimination is involved.
- Complaints can also be filed with the **National Human Rights Commission (NHRC)** or the **Karnataka State Human Rights Commission**.
- Civil suits for damages due to discriminatory practices are also permissible.

## 7. Stamp Duty and Registration of Rental Agreements

### Mandatory Registration

Under the **Indian Registration Act, 1908** and the **Karnataka Stamp Act, 1957**:

- Rental agreements for a period of **12 months or more** must be **compulsorily registered** with the Sub-Registrar.
- Agreements for **less than 12 months** are not required to be registered but should be executed on **stamp paper** of appropriate value.
- **Unregistered agreements** for periods of 12 months or more are **not admissible as evidence** in court.

### Stamp Duty Rates in Karnataka

| Lease Duration | Stamp Duty |
|---------------|-----------|
| Up to 12 months | 1% of total annual rent |
| 1 to 10 years | 1% of total rent for the entire period |
| Above 10 years | 2% of total rent for the entire period, plus 1% of any advance/deposit |

- **Registration fee**: Typically 1% of the annual rent, subject to a minimum of ₹500.
- **e-Stamping**: Karnataka allows procurement of stamp paper through the **SHCIL (Stock Holding Corporation of India Ltd) e-Stamping** portal.

### Online Registration (Kaveri Portal)

- Karnataka has digitized the registration process through the **Kaveri Online Services** portal (https://kaverionline.karnataka.gov.in).
- Both landlord and tenant must be present for registration, or an authorized power-of-attorney holder may attend.
- **Biometric verification** (Aadhaar-based) is required during the registration process.
