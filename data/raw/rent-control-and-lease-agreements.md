# Karnataka Rent Control and Lease Agreement Regulations

## Overview

The rental market in Karnataka is regulated by the **Karnataka Rent Act, 1999**, the **Transfer of Property Act, 1882**, the **Indian Registration Act, 1908**, and the **Karnataka Stamp Act, 1957**. These laws establish the framework for rent determination, rent increases, lease agreements, and the rights and obligations of landlords and tenants. The central **Model Tenancy Act, 2021** has been recommended for adoption but Karnataka continues to operate under its existing state legislation.

---

## 1. Rent Determination and Fair Rent

### Concept of Fair Rent

Under the Karnataka Rent Act, 1999, the concept of **"fair rent"** is central to rent regulation:

- Either the landlord or the tenant may apply to the **Rent Authority** to fix the **fair rent** for the premises.
- Once fair rent is fixed, the landlord cannot charge rent in excess of the fair rent.
- Fair rent is determined based on:
  - **Cost of construction** or the **market value of the building**.
  - **Prevailing rental rates** in the locality for similar premises.
  - **Amenities provided** — such as parking, lift, security, water supply, and generator backup.
  - **Age of the building** — depreciation is factored in.
  - **Location** — proximity to commercial areas, transport hubs, schools, and hospitals.

### Standard Rent Calculation

The fair rent is typically calculated as a **percentage of the total cost of construction** (including the proportionate cost of land):

| Type of Premises | Annual Fair Rent (Approximate) |
|-----------------|-------------------------------|
| Residential | 7.5% to 10% of the total cost of construction |
| Commercial | 10% to 12% of the total cost of construction |

> **Note**: These are indicative rates. The actual fair rent is determined by the Rent Authority based on evidence presented by both parties.

### Applicability

- Fair rent provisions primarily apply to **older properties** and properties falling within the purview of the Rent Act.
- **Newer constructions** (particularly those built after the Rent Act came into force) and properties with rents agreed upon in a registered lease agreement are generally governed by the **contractual rent** agreed between the parties.
- In practice, most modern rental transactions in Bengaluru and other cities operate on **market rent** determined by mutual agreement.

## 2. Rent Increases

### Permissible Rent Increases Under the Karnataka Rent Act

- A landlord may increase rent only in accordance with the provisions of the rental agreement and the Rent Act.
- If a fair rent has been fixed, the landlord may apply for a **revision of fair rent** after a period of **five years** from the date of the last fixation.
- The increase upon revision is generally limited to a **reasonable percentage** determined by the Rent Authority, taking into account:
  - Increase in **municipal taxes** and property taxes.
  - Cost of **improvements and repairs** carried out by the landlord.
  - General **inflation** and increase in the cost of living.

### Contractual Rent Increases

For properties governed by a **registered lease agreement** (not subject to fair rent fixation):

- The lease agreement typically includes a clause for **annual rent escalation**, commonly ranging from **5% to 10%** per annum.
- A **5% annual increase** is the most common escalation clause in residential leases in Karnataka.
- The rent increase takes effect on the **anniversary of the lease commencement date** or as specified in the agreement.
- The landlord must provide **prior written notice** of the rent increase as stipulated in the agreement (commonly 1–3 months before the increase).

### Notice Requirements for Rent Increases

| Tenancy Type | Notice Period for Rent Increase |
|-------------|-------------------------------|
| Month-to-month | 15 days' written notice |
| Fixed-term lease | As per the escalation clause in the agreement |
| Yearly tenancy | 6 months' notice (ending with the year of tenancy) |

### Tenant's Right to Challenge

- If a tenant believes the rent increase is **excessive or unreasonable**, they may:
  - Apply to the **Rent Authority** for fixation of fair rent.
  - Challenge the increase in **Civil Court** if it violates the terms of the rental agreement.
  - Seek relief through **consumer forums** if the increase amounts to an unfair trade practice.

## 3. Lease Agreement Requirements

### Types of Rental Agreements in Karnataka

1. **Lease Deed (Registered)**: A formal agreement registered with the Sub-Registrar, mandatory for leases of **11 months or more**.
2. **Rental Agreement (Unregistered)**: Commonly executed for a period of **11 months** on stamp paper to avoid compulsory registration.
3. **Leave and License Agreement**: A license to occupy (not a lease), often used for short-term arrangements. The licensee has fewer rights than a tenant.
4. **Month-to-Month Tenancy**: An oral or written arrangement without a fixed term, renewed monthly by conduct.

### Why 11-Month Agreements Are Common

- Under the **Indian Registration Act, 1908**, lease agreements for a period of **12 months or more** must be **compulsorily registered**.
- Registration involves stamp duty, registration fees, and the presence of both parties — adding cost and effort.
- To avoid these requirements, landlords commonly execute **11-month rental agreements** on non-judicial stamp paper, which are legally valid but provide somewhat **weaker legal protection** compared to registered leases.
- However, an unregistered 11-month agreement is still **admissible as evidence of the terms of the tenancy** (though not as proof of the lease itself).

### Essential Clauses in a Karnataka Rental Agreement

A comprehensive rental agreement in Karnataka should include:

| Clause | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Parties** | Full names, addresses, and identification details (Aadhaar, PAN) of landlord and tenant |
| **Property description** | Complete address, door number, floor, area in sq. ft., survey/site number |
| **Term** | Duration of the agreement (e.g., 11 months) and commencement date |
| **Rent** | Monthly rent amount, due date, and mode of payment |
| **Security deposit** | Amount of deposit, terms for deduction, and timeline for return |
| **Rent escalation** | Annual percentage increase (typically 5%) |
| **Maintenance charges** | Monthly maintenance (if applicable), who bears it, and payment terms |
| **Utilities** | Responsibility for electricity, water, gas, internet, and other utility bills |
| **Purpose of use** | Residential, commercial, or specific permitted use |
| **Lock-in period** | Minimum period during which neither party may terminate (commonly 3–6 months) |
| **Notice period** | Notice required for termination by either party (commonly 1–2 months) |
| **Subletting** | Whether subletting is permitted (usually prohibited without consent) |
| **Pets** | Whether pets are allowed and any conditions |
| **Alterations** | Restrictions on structural or material changes |
| **Maintenance responsibilities** | Division of maintenance obligations between landlord and tenant |
| **Inventory list** | List of furniture, fixtures, and appliances provided by the landlord |
| **Dispute resolution** | Mediation, arbitration, or court jurisdiction (typically the local court) |
| **Governing law** | Applicable laws (Karnataka Rent Act, Transfer of Property Act, etc.) |

### Mandatory Disclosures

While Karnataka does not have a formal disclosure law comparable to other jurisdictions, it is best practice for landlords to disclose:

- **Structural defects** or pending repairs.
- **Pending litigation** related to the property.
- **Encumbrances** — mortgages, liens, or charges on the property.
- **Property tax arrears**.
- **Approved building plan** and occupancy certificate.
- **Water supply source** — Cauvery connection, borewell, or tanker.
- **Power backup** availability (generator/inverter).
- **Society/Association rules** — restrictions imposed by apartment owners' associations.

## 4. Stamp Duty and Registration

### Stamp Duty for Rental Agreements in Karnataka

Under the **Karnataka Stamp Act, 1957**:

| Agreement Type | Stamp Duty |
|---------------|-----------|
| **Rental agreement (up to 12 months)** | 1% of the total annual rent + deposit amount |
| **Lease deed (1–10 years)** | 1% of the total rent for the entire period + refundable deposit |
| **Lease deed (above 10 years)** | 2% of the total rent for the entire period + 1% of non-refundable deposit |

### Registration Fees

| Component | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| **Registration fee** | 1% of the annual rent, minimum ₹500 |
| **Biometric verification fee** | ₹50–₹100 per person |
| **Scanning and processing** | ₹50–₹200 |

### Process for Registration

1. **Procure e-Stamp paper** from an authorized vendor or through the **SHCIL portal**.
2. **Draft the agreement** with all essential clauses.
3. **Book an appointment** on the **Kaveri Online Services** portal (https://kaverionline.karnataka.gov.in).
4. **Visit the Sub-Registrar's office** with:
   - Both parties (landlord and tenant) with valid photo ID (Aadhaar card).
   - Two witnesses with valid photo ID.
   - The agreement on appropriate stamp paper.
   - Passport-size photographs of all parties.
   - Property documents (tax paid receipt, khata certificate, etc.).
5. **Biometric verification** (Aadhaar-based) is conducted at the office.
6. **Registered copy** is returned to the parties, typically within **2–5 working days**.

### Consequences of Non-Registration

- An unregistered agreement for 12 months or more is **not admissible as evidence** of the lease in court (Section 49, Registration Act).
- However, it may be admitted as **evidence of the nature of possession** or as a **collateral transaction**.
- The tenant may face difficulties in proving the terms of tenancy in a dispute.
- The landlord may face issues in eviction proceedings.

## 5. Lock-In Period and Early Termination

### Lock-In Period

- A **lock-in period** is a clause in the rental agreement during which **neither party may terminate** the agreement.
- Common lock-in periods in Karnataka are **3 months, 6 months, or 12 months**.
- If a tenant vacates during the lock-in period, the landlord may:
  - **Forfeit the security deposit** (partially or fully, as per the agreement).
  - Claim **rent for the remaining lock-in period** as damages.
- If a landlord forces the tenant to vacate during the lock-in period, the tenant may claim:
  - **Compensation equivalent to rent** for the remaining lock-in period.
  - **Refund of the full security deposit**.

### Notice Period for Termination

| Party | Typical Notice Period |
|-------|---------------------|
| Tenant (after lock-in) | 1–2 months' written notice |
| Landlord (after lock-in) | 1–2 months' written notice |
| Month-to-month tenancy | 15 days' notice (per Transfer of Property Act) |

### Early Termination Grounds

Either party may seek early termination without penalty if:

- The other party has **materially breached** the agreement.
- The premises have become **uninhabitable** due to natural calamity or structural failure.
- The tenant is **transferred to another city** (if the agreement includes a transfer clause).
- **Mutual written consent** is obtained from both parties.

## 6. Subletting and Assignment

### General Rule

Under the Karnataka Rent Act, 1999:

- A tenant may **not sublet** the premises or any part thereof without the **written consent** of the landlord.
- **Unauthorized subletting** is a valid ground for eviction.
- Even if the subtenant vacates, the act of unauthorized subletting remains a ground for eviction.

### When Subletting Is Permitted

- If the rental agreement **expressly permits** subletting, the tenant may sublet subject to the conditions specified.
- The landlord may impose conditions such as:
  - Prior written approval for each subtenant.
  - Screening of the proposed subtenant.
  - A reasonable increase in rent to reflect the subletting.
  - Direct responsibility of the original tenant for the subtenant's obligations.

### Assignment of Lease

- **Assignment** (transfer of the entire lease to a new tenant) is generally prohibited without the landlord's consent.
- An assignment without consent is voidable at the landlord's option and may constitute grounds for eviction.

## 7. Rent Payment During Disputes

### Depositing Rent in Court

If a dispute arises between the landlord and tenant (e.g., the landlord refuses to accept rent, or there is a disagreement about the rent amount):

- The tenant should continue paying rent by **depositing it with the Rent Authority or Civil Court**.
- This protects the tenant from eviction on the ground of **non-payment of rent**.
- The deposit must be made **regularly** (monthly) and should include any arrears.

### Consequences of Non-Payment During Dispute

- If the tenant fails to deposit rent during the pendency of a dispute, the court may:
  - **Strike off the tenant's defence** in eviction proceedings.
  - Pass an **ex-parte order** in favour of the landlord.
  - Treat the non-payment as an **independent ground for eviction**.

## 8. Tenant Protections Specific to Karnataka

### Protection Against Arbitrary Eviction

- A tenant who has been in **continuous occupation** of the premises cannot be evicted except on the grounds specified in the Karnataka Rent Act.
- Even after the expiry of the lease, the tenant has the right to continue in occupation as a **statutory tenant** (tenant holding over), subject to the provisions of the Rent Act.

### Inheritance of Tenancy Rights

Under the Karnataka Rent Act:

- Upon the **death of a tenant**, the tenancy rights may devolve on the tenant's:
  - **Spouse**
  - **Son or daughter** residing with the tenant
  - **Legal heirs** as per the applicable personal law
- The landlord cannot evict the legal heir solely on the ground that the original tenant has passed away.

### Right to Essential Services

- The tenant has the right to uninterrupted **essential services** including:
  - Water supply
  - Electricity
  - Sanitation and drainage
  - Lift/elevator service (in apartment buildings)
- The landlord cannot withhold these services as a means of pressuring the tenant.

### Tenant's Right to Receipts and Records

- The tenant has the right to demand and receive:
  - **Rent receipts** for every payment made.
  - A copy of the **rental agreement**.
  - **Utility bills** (if the landlord collects payments on behalf of the tenant).
  - **Maintenance charge breakdowns** (in apartment complexes).

## 9. Special Provisions

### Government and Institutional Tenancies

- Properties owned by the **Karnataka State Government** or local bodies are governed by separate rules, including the **Karnataka Public Premises (Eviction of Unauthorised Occupants) Act, 1974**.
- Eviction from government premises follows a **summary procedure** with limited defences available to the occupant.

### Agricultural Land and Rural Tenancies

- Agricultural tenancies in Karnataka are governed by the **Karnataka Land Reforms Act, 1961**, which provides extensive protections to agricultural tenants, including:
  - **Security of tenure** — an agricultural tenant cannot be evicted except on specific grounds.
  - **Right to purchase** — tenants may have the right to acquire ownership of the land they cultivate.
  - **Ceiling on rent** — agricultural rent is capped at a percentage of the gross produce.

### Paying Guest (PG) Arrangements

- PG accommodations are typically treated as **license arrangements**, not leases.
- The occupant (PG resident) has **fewer legal protections** than a tenant.
- PG operators in Bengaluru must comply with **BBMP regulations** and may need to register with local police stations.
- The **Karnataka Home Stay/PG Establishment Rules** require registration and compliance with safety and hygiene standards.

## 10. Dispute Resolution Mechanisms

### Available Forums

| Forum | Jurisdiction | Typical Timeline |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Rent Authority** | Rent disputes, eviction, fair rent fixation | 1–3 years |
| **Civil Court** | Lease disputes, damages, specific performance | 2–5 years |
| **Consumer Forum** | Deficiency in service, unfair practices | 6 months–2 years |
| **Lok Adalat** | Settlement of disputes by mutual consent | Single sitting |
| **Mediation Centre** | Pre-litigation and court-referred mediation | 1–3 months |
| **High Court of Karnataka** | Appeals, writs, revision petitions | 1–5 years |

### Lok Adalat and Mediation

- **Lok Adalats** conducted by the Karnataka State Legal Services Authority offer a **free, fast, and binding** resolution mechanism.
- **Mediation** through the Bengaluru Mediation Centre (attached to the High Court of Karnataka) is increasingly encouraged for rental disputes.
- Both forums help avoid the **lengthy litigation process** that is common in Karnataka courts.

### Police Involvement

- Police may intervene in rental disputes only when there is a **cognizable offence** (e.g., criminal trespass, assault, intimidation).
- For civil disputes (e.g., rent arrears, deposit refund), the police will typically direct the parties to approach the **civil court or Rent Authority**.
