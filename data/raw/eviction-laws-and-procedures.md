# Karnataka Eviction Laws and Procedures

## Overview

Eviction of tenants in Karnataka is governed by the **Karnataka Rent Act, 1999**, the **Transfer of Property Act, 1882**, and applicable provisions of the **Code of Civil Procedure, 1908**. A landlord cannot evict a tenant without following due legal process. Self-help evictions (forcible removal, locking out, or disconnecting services) are illegal and may attract criminal penalties.

---

## 1. Grounds for Eviction Under the Karnataka Rent Act, 1999

### Section 21 — Grounds on Which a Landlord May Seek Eviction

A landlord may file an eviction petition before the **Rent Authority** (or Civil Court where the Rent Authority is not constituted) on the following grounds:

#### (a) Non-Payment of Rent

- The tenant has failed to pay rent for a period of **two months** after it became due.
- The landlord must issue a **written notice** demanding payment and allow the tenant at least **15 days** to clear the arrears before filing for eviction.
- If the tenant pays all arrears, including any costs assessed by the court, **within the time granted by the court**, the eviction application may be dismissed.

#### (b) Misuse of Premises

- The tenant is using the premises for a **purpose other than** what was agreed upon in the rental agreement.
- Example: Using a residential property for commercial purposes without the landlord's consent.

#### (c) Causing Nuisance or Annoyance

- The tenant is causing **nuisance or annoyance** to adjoining or neighbouring occupiers.
- The tenant is using the premises for **immoral or illegal purposes**.

#### (d) Damage to Property

- The tenant has caused or is likely to cause **substantial damage** to the premises, whether wilfully or through gross negligence.
- The damage must go beyond **normal wear and tear**.

#### (e) Material Alteration Without Consent

- The tenant has made **material alterations** to the premises without the landlord's written consent.
- Material alterations include structural changes, partitioning, additions, or demolition of any part of the premises.

#### (f) Landlord's Bona Fide Personal Need

- The landlord **reasonably and bona fide requires** the premises for their own occupation, or for the occupation of any person for whose benefit the premises are held.
- The landlord must prove that the need is **genuine** and not merely a pretext to evict the tenant.
- The court will consider whether the landlord has **other reasonably suitable accommodation** available.

#### (g) Reconstruction or Demolition

- The landlord requires the premises to be **demolished for reconstruction** or for carrying out substantial repairs that cannot be done without the tenant vacating.
- The landlord must obtain necessary **municipal approvals** (BBMP building plan sanction).
- The tenant may have the **right of first refusal** to re-occupy the premises after reconstruction at a fair rent.

#### (h) Tenant Has Acquired Alternative Accommodation

- The tenant has **built, acquired, or been allotted** a suitable residence in the same city or town.

#### (i) Expiry of Agreed Period

- The agreed period of tenancy has **expired** and the landlord has issued proper notice.
- For month-to-month tenancies, the landlord must give **15 days' notice** ending with the month of tenancy.

#### (j) Subletting Without Consent

- The tenant has **sublet the premises** or any part thereof without the landlord's written consent.
- Unauthorized subletting is a valid ground for eviction even if the subtenant is no longer in occupation.

## 2. Eviction Notice Requirements

### Types of Notices

| Notice Type | Duration | When Used |
|------------|----------|-----------|
| **Notice for Non-Payment** | 15 days | Before filing eviction for rent arrears |
| **Notice to Quit (Month-to-Month)** | 15 days | Terminating a monthly tenancy |
| **Notice to Quit (Yearly Tenancy)** | 6 months | Terminating a tenancy from year to year (ending with the year of tenancy) |
| **Notice Under Section 106, Transfer of Property Act** | As per lease terms or statutory default | General notice for lease termination |

### Service of Notice

The notice must be served in one of the following legally recognized methods:

1. **Personal delivery**: Handing the notice directly to the tenant.
2. **Registered post with acknowledgement due (RPAD)**: Sent to the tenant's address. The postal acknowledgement serves as proof of delivery.
3. **Affixing on the premises**: If the tenant refuses to accept or cannot be found, the notice may be affixed to a **conspicuous part of the property** in the presence of witnesses.
4. **Through an advocate**: A notice sent through an advocate (lawyer's notice) on behalf of the landlord.

### Essential Contents of a Valid Notice

A valid eviction notice must contain:

- **Full name and address** of the landlord and tenant.
- **Description of the premises** (address, survey number, door number).
- **Ground(s) for eviction** clearly stated.
- **Period allowed** for the tenant to comply or vacate.
- **Date of notice** and **signature** of the landlord or authorized advocate.
- **Demand for arrears** (if the ground is non-payment), specifying the exact amount due.

## 3. Eviction Proceedings

### Filing Before the Rent Authority

Under the Karnataka Rent Act, 1999:

1. **Petition filing**: The landlord files an eviction petition before the **Rent Authority** having jurisdiction over the area where the property is situated.
2. **Jurisdiction**: The Rent Authority is typically the **Munsiff Court** or a specially designated officer appointed by the state government.
3. **Court fee**: The prescribed court fee must be paid at the time of filing.

### Process Flow

```
Notice to Tenant → Tenant's Response/Default → Filing of Eviction Petition →
Service of Summons → Tenant's Written Statement → Evidence/Hearing →
Order by Rent Authority → Appeal (if any) → Execution of Order
```

### Step-by-Step Procedure

#### Step 1: Issue Notice
- Serve a valid notice as described above.
- Wait for the notice period to expire.

#### Step 2: File Eviction Petition
- If the tenant does not vacate or remedy the breach, the landlord files an eviction petition with the Rent Authority.
- The petition must include:
  - Details of the tenancy (agreement, duration, rent).
  - The ground(s) for eviction.
  - Copies of the notice served and proof of service.
  - Supporting documents (rent receipts, photographs, municipal notices, etc.).

#### Step 3: Service of Summons
- The Rent Authority issues summons to the tenant.
- The tenant must appear and file a **written statement** (defence) within the time granted by the court (typically **30 days**).

#### Step 4: Trial and Evidence
- Both parties present their evidence, including **oral testimony** and **documentary evidence**.
- Cross-examination of witnesses is permitted.
- The Rent Authority may also conduct a **local inspection** of the premises if necessary.

#### Step 5: Order
- After hearing both parties, the Rent Authority passes an order either:
  - **Allowing the eviction** and directing the tenant to vacate within a specified period (usually **1 to 3 months**).
  - **Dismissing the petition** if the grounds are not established.

#### Step 6: Appeal
- An aggrieved party may file an **appeal** before the **Appellate Authority** (typically the District Court or the Principal Civil Judge) within **30 days** of the order.
- A further **revision petition** may be filed before the **High Court of Karnataka** under Section 115 of the Code of Civil Procedure.

#### Step 7: Execution
- If the tenant fails to vacate after the order becomes final, the landlord may apply for **execution of the order**.
- The court may issue a **warrant of possession**, and the tenant may be physically evicted through the **court bailiff** with police assistance if necessary.

## 4. Tenant's Defences Against Eviction

### Common Defences

Tenants may raise the following defences in eviction proceedings:

1. **Invalid notice**: The notice was defective in form, content, or service.
2. **Payment of arrears**: The tenant has paid or is willing to pay all rent arrears and costs (available as a defence under the Karnataka Rent Act for first-time defaults).
3. **No bona fide need**: The landlord's claim of personal need is not genuine, or the landlord has alternative accommodation available.
4. **Tenant's improvements**: The tenant has made significant improvements to the property with the landlord's consent, which should be considered.
5. **Waiver**: The landlord has waived the breach by accepting rent after knowledge of the violation.
6. **Retaliatory eviction**: The eviction is in retaliation for the tenant's complaint to municipal authorities or exercise of legal rights.
7. **Hardship**: The tenant will suffer **greater hardship** from eviction than the landlord would suffer from the refusal of the eviction order (considered by courts in balancing equities).
8. **Absence of landlord-tenant relationship**: The occupant claims to be a **licensee, caretaker, or co-owner** rather than a tenant.

### Protection Against Eviction During Pendency

- During the pendency of eviction proceedings, the tenant is entitled to continue in occupation, provided the tenant **deposits rent regularly** (either to the landlord or into court, as directed).
- Failure to deposit rent during proceedings may result in the court **striking off the tenant's defence**.

## 5. Illegal Eviction and Self-Help Remedies

### What Constitutes Illegal Eviction

The following actions by a landlord constitute **illegal eviction** under Karnataka law:

- **Forcible removal** of the tenant from the premises.
- **Changing locks** and denying the tenant access.
- **Disconnecting utilities** — electricity, water, gas, or sewage.
- **Removing doors, windows, or the tenant's belongings**.
- **Threatening, intimidating, or assaulting** the tenant to compel vacation.
- **Engaging goons or muscle power** to force the tenant out.

### Criminal Liability

A landlord who resorts to illegal eviction may face:

- **Criminal trespass** under Section 329 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023 (replacing IPC Section 441).
- **Criminal intimidation** under Section 351 of BNS, 2023 (replacing IPC Section 503).
- **Hurt or assault** charges under Sections 115–117 of BNS, 2023 (replacing IPC Sections 319–323).
- **Mischief** (damage to property) under Section 324 of BNS, 2023 (replacing IPC Section 425).

### Tenant's Remedies

If subjected to illegal eviction, the tenant may:

1. **File an FIR** at the nearest police station.
2. Approach the **Civil Court** for an **injunction** to restrain the landlord and restore possession.
3. File a **complaint before the Rent Authority**.
4. Seek **compensation for damages** — including cost of alternative accommodation, damage to belongings, and mental agony — through a civil suit.
5. In urgent cases, approach the **High Court of Karnataka** under Article 226 of the Constitution for a writ of mandamus.

## 6. Eviction of Unauthorized Occupants and Trespassers

### Distinction from Tenants

- An **unauthorized occupant** or trespasser does not have the protections of the Karnataka Rent Act.
- The landlord may file a **suit for possession** under the Code of Civil Procedure or seek police assistance.
- However, the landlord must still follow **legal process** and cannot resort to self-help.

### Summary Suit for Possession

- Under **Order XXXVII** of the CPC, a landlord may file a **summary suit** for recovery of possession in clear-cut cases, which provides a faster resolution.
- The court may grant possession within **a few months** in uncontested cases.

## 7. Eviction Under the Model Tenancy Act, 2021

### Key Differences (If Adopted by Karnataka)

The central **Model Tenancy Act, 2021** proposes significant changes to the eviction framework:

| Aspect | Karnataka Rent Act, 1999 | Model Tenancy Act, 2021 |
|--------|-------------------------|------------------------|
| **Adjudicating body** | Rent Authority / Civil Court | Rent Authority → Rent Court → Rent Tribunal |
| **Timeline for disposal** | No fixed timeline (often years) | **60 days** for Rent Authority orders |
| **Grounds for eviction** | Listed under Section 21 | Similar grounds, with added clarity |
| **Overstaying penalty** | Standard damages | **Double rent for 2 months**, then **4x rent** thereafter |
| **Subletting** | Ground for eviction | Ground for eviction with enhanced penalties |
| **Digital filing** | Limited | Fully digital filing envisioned |

### Current Status in Karnataka

- As of 2024, Karnataka has **not formally adopted** the Model Tenancy Act, 2021.
- The existing **Karnataka Rent Act, 1999** continues to govern landlord-tenant relationships.
- The state government has been deliberating on amendments to align with the central model law.

## 8. Practical Timelines for Eviction in Karnataka

### Typical Duration

Eviction proceedings in Karnataka can be **lengthy**. Below are approximate timelines:

| Stage | Approximate Duration |
|-------|---------------------|
| Notice period | 15 days to 6 months |
| Filing and summons | 1–2 months |
| Tenant's written statement | 1–2 months |
| Evidence and hearing | 6–18 months |
| Rent Authority order | 1–3 years (total) |
| Appeal (District Court) | 1–2 years |
| Revision (High Court) | 1–3 years |
| Execution of order | 1–6 months |

> **Note**: In practice, contested eviction cases in Bengaluru and other cities in Karnataka may take **3 to 7 years** from filing to final resolution, including appeals. Mediation and negotiated settlements are strongly encouraged.

### Fast-Track Options

- **Mediation**: The Karnataka Mediation and Conciliation Centre and Lok Adalats offer quicker resolution.
- **Lok Adalat**: Cases can be settled in a single sitting with legally binding orders.
- **Consent orders**: If both parties agree, the court can record a consent order for eviction on agreed terms.
